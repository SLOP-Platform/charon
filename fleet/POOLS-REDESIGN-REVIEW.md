# Adversarial review — POOLS-REDESIGN-ADR

**Reviewer stance:** try to BEAT the proposal. Read-only. Grounded in the ADR + `pools-analysis.md`, `pools-origin-attribution.md`, `research-gateway-landscape.md`, and the live evidence in `model-scorecard.tsv` + `HANDOFF-2026-07-06.md`.

**VERDICT: REWORK** — not because the structural diagnosis is wrong (it's excellent) but because the ADR commits, as *core* Phases 1–2, to the single most expensive and least-supported layer (per-work-class capability grades + Arch-Router), while the cheaper subset it explicitly rejects (Option A+) delivers ~90% of the realizable value on data that actually exists today. The decision should be **inverted and re-sequenced**, not blessed. The tier/health/real-cost structural fix is a clear SHIP; the work-class capability engine is a REWORK-or-defer.

---

## The single strongest argument AGAINST the proposal

**On the data that exists today, the ADR's distinctive feature — per-work-class, capability-aware routing — is inert, and its own proof-of-effect gate cannot detect that it's inert.**

The benchmark the ADR names as its "primary quality signal" does not meaningfully differentiate models. In `model-scorecard.tsv` all four fully-scored models score **100 on nearly every S0–S6 section** (gpt-5.4: 100/100/100/75/100/100/100 → composite 95.8; glm-5.2: straight 100s; kimi-k2.6 and hy3 nearly the same). The composite is `mean(S1–S6)` and time/cost are *explicitly excluded from the base grade* (HANDOFF L48) — used only as a tiebreak. So even for the 4 covered models, the `work_class_grades` columns are a wall of near-ties, and the *actual* discriminator is efficiency (cost/latency/health) — which is exactly what a cheap "template + real cost_rank + health ledger" delivers **without** the grades table, the classifier, or the benchmark dependency.

Worse, the benchmark that would fold efficiency into a discriminating score — **benchmark-v2 — does not exist yet** (HANDOFF L47: "operator-approved design, to build next session"). The ADR's entire quality signal is a v1 that saturates at 100 plus a v2 that is vapor. Building routing-brain machinery on top of a signal that (a) barely discriminates and (b) isn't built is the SR-6 failure mode wearing a fancier schema — the exact thing this ADR claims to prevent.

## Ranked issues

### 1. [SHIP-BLOCKER] Coverage: tier itself is undefined for ~197/201 routes, not just the grades
A tier is defined (ADR §"The 4 tiers") as "membership derived from a model's composite grade crossing a documented cut." Only **4 models** have a composite. So for ~197 routes **there is no tier at all** — not just thin grades. The routing algorithm's first step on failover is `tier = grades[primary_model].tier`; if the primary is one of the 197, that lookup is null and the whole "best-in-tier" path has nothing to stand on. The ADR's "honest risk" bullet frames this as "work-class grades thin" — that undersells it: it's **tier-unknown for 98% of the catalog.** The fallbacks are a false bridge or a regression:
- **Public benchmarks (LMArena/MMLU/HumanEval)** measure general chat/knowledge/single-file coding, **not** Charon's agentic S0–S6 work-classes (money-path failover correctness, ci-infra pipefail, anti-dodge test discipline). Mapping an MMLU percentile onto an S2-routing tier is unvalidated — a genuine false bridge, and the ADR asserts the mapping exists without showing it.
- **Generalist default** "never worse than today" is only true if it reproduces today's ordering. But today's ordering is the hand-tuned `pools.json` array order (the zen-demote work). A generalist mean grade does **not** encode that operator tuning, so the fallback can be *worse* than today for exactly the pools the operator hand-ordered. "Never worse" is asserted, not shown.
- **Fix:** Decouple *tier assignment* from *benchmark composite*. Tier must have a cheap, complete, hand-or-catalog-seeded source (the existing 15-entry `model_catalog.tier_hint` + `tiers.json` already do this for a subset) so 100% of routes have a tier on day 1; let the benchmark *refine* tier for the handful it covers. Otherwise the feature is gated on benchmarking ~181 more models through a ~1hr harness each — an effort that "may never cover the catalog," and the ADR should say so in the Decision, not bury it in risks.

### 2. [MAJOR — cheaper option wins for now] Option A+ beats the chosen design on current data
The ADR steelmans and rejects "generate the 50 pools from a template." But it attacks a **strawman** of Option A (a dumb generator that emits 50 pools with no priority signal). The real competitor is **Option A+**: template-generated membership (kills the 82% boilerplate and INV-P0 violation) **+ real `cost_rank` from a pricing import** (kills the inert-cost/list-order hack) **+ a live-health tiebreak** (the Unify "who-wins-now" signal) **+ optionally a coarse 4-tier *label*** hand/catalog-seeded for cascade grouping. Option A+ delivers every benefit the ADR's own "why it beats Option A" paragraph claims is unique to the 4-tier design — collapsed authoring cost, explicit cost home, no array-order hack — **except** per-work-class capability grades, which (per the strongest-argument section) do nothing on today's data. Option A+ is ~10–20% of the risk: no classifier in the hot path, no benchmark-coverage dependency, no false MMLU→S-class bridge. **Honest call: for the next several months, Option A+ wins.** The 4-tier + grades design only pulls ahead *after* benchmark-v2 exists and discriminates across a meaningful fraction of the catalog — which is unproven and may not happen. The ADR's rejection of "do less" is therefore **unsound as written**, because it rejects the wrong (weaker) version of "less."

### 3. [MAJOR] Mechanization gate (a) is gameable and does NOT test the distinctive feature
Gate (a) fails if `pct_differentiated < 90%`, where differentiated = `work_class_grades not all-null AND cost_rank != inert_default`. Two independent problems:
- **It's blocking-impossible or trivially-gamed.** Honestly filling `work_class_grades` for 90% of 201 routes requires benchmarking ~181 models (won't happen soon → gate blocks rollout forever). The escape hatch is a uniform backfill (stamp every model the same generalist/public number) — which makes "not all-null" trivially true while every model carries an identical grade → **zero differentiation** → routing identical to Option A+. So the gate passes on a table that does none of the capability routing the ADR exists to add. This is *precisely* the SR-6 gaming the reviewer was told to hunt for: the gate checks presence, not effect.
- **It measures the wrong thing.** It proves cost/grade *fields are populated*, not that capability routing *ever changes a decision*. The gate that would actually prevent an inert rollout: **"for ≥X% of (work-class, tier) cases, the capability-ranked winner differs from the cost-only winner"** — i.e. does the work-class machinery ever override what free-first-then-cost would have picked anyway? On current all-100 data that number is ~0%, which would correctly fail the feature as inert. The ADR should adopt *that* gate; as written, gate (a) is prose dressed as a test.

### 3b. [MODERATE] Pricing source is unknown — gate (a)'s cost half rests on an unsolved problem
HANDOFF L53 (PRICING-SOURCE CORRECTION): `/data/models.json` has **no** `cost_input/cost_output`, the "pricing in models.json" assumption is stale, and *nobody currently knows where the gateway's per-token pricing actually lives*. The ADR's models.dev import ("makes cost_rank real, the fix SR-6 left inert") assumes a clean injection point that hasn't been located. Until that's found, the cost half of gate (a) is also unbuildable. **Fix:** the "locate pricing" task (already a HANDOFF next-step) is a hard prerequisite of Phase 1, not an implementation detail.

### 4. [MODERATE] Work-class classifier: unproven taxonomy + hot-path latency + low payoff
Arch-Router's 93.17% is against *its own* taxonomy, not S0–S6 (ADR concedes this). A 1.5B local inference on the **failover path** adds latency to the exact moment the user is already waiting on a retry, and adds a moving part (a served model + its own health) to a "single Python venv, no new runtime dependency" product — arguably straining the standalone constraint. But the sharper point: given issue #1's all-100 grades, **even a *perfect* work-class classification changes almost no routing decision today**, because the per-class grades don't diverge. High cost, near-zero current payoff. Defer behind proof the grades discriminate by work-class. The GENERALIST fallback is the right instinct — but if GENERALIST is what runs 98% of the time (issue #1), then you've built the classifier scaffolding for the 2% and the honest default *is* Option A+.

### 5. [MODERATE] Migration: Phase 3 has a routing-breaks window, and revertibility is asserted not shown
- Phases 1–2 (dark launch, shadow) are genuinely safe and independently revertible — good design. But **Phase 3 flips the flag AND regenerates `pools.json` from 50 → 4 sets AND migrates the setup API/dashboard CRUD AND rewires the 3 lists in one cutover.** That is four high-blast changes fused; the ADR's own §"honest risks" calls Phase 3 "the highest-blast-radius step." Splitting the *routing* flip (reversible: flip back) from the *schema regeneration* (destructive: 50-pool shape is gone) is necessary — otherwise "rollback" means restoring a `/data` snapshot while live, which is downtime, not a revert.
- The incompatible `pools.py` ACP loader (mandatory `"agent"` field) is correctly flagged, but the ADR only says "keep the separation explicit." Under a *single grades table as SINGLE source* (the stated goal + gate c), the risk is that the unification pressure points the ACP loader at agent-less rows and crashes it — the exact failure `_tier_pools`'s docstring warns about. The ADR needs a concrete assertion (a test) that the ACP path is *never* fed grades-table rows, not a promise.
- Gate (c) requires opencode-mirror and CLI-catalog to become *generated views* checksummed to `grades_table@<hash>`. But the opencode mirror lives in `~/.config/opencode/` on the operator's machine, outside the repo, read only at process start (needs full `pkill`). A CI checksum gate cannot see or enforce an out-of-repo, restart-gated file. Gate (c) is partly unenforceable-in-CI as written.

### 6. [MODERATE] "4 tiers" is asserted, not derived; and it may be the wrong count
The ADR leans hard on "these 4 labels already exist and are live-validated." But they're validated as *output bins of one composite*, on 4 models, 3 of which land in the same top bin (Frontier). There is **no evidence the catalog actually populates 4 usefully-distinct tiers** — the live data so far is "everything good is Frontier." The cascade Frontier→Strong→Capable→Basic assumes a smooth quality gradient the data doesn't yet show. Meanwhile the *existing* `model_catalog.tier_hint` uses **3** (high/med/low). Committing to exactly 4 before the catalog is scored enough to show 4 real strata is premature. Not a blocker, but the "4 is right" claim is unearned.

### 7. [MINOR — outside-the-box] Simpler shapes the ADR underweights
- **openrouter/auto for one tier:** dismissed wholesale as a *foundation*, correctly. But as the *Frontier-tier fallback specifically* — where "just get me a top model, I don't care which" is exactly the semantic — it's a near-free, already-integrated option that sidesteps benchmarking the frontier. Worth keeping as a per-tier option, not only a bare passthrough id.
- **Two signals, not three:** the ADR's own evidence collapses "capability grade" and "cost/health" into mostly the latter. A design with just **(coarse tier label) × (cost+health ledger)** — dropping per-work-class grades until proven — is the real minimal shape and is strictly inside the ADR's Phase-1/Phase-4 scope. That *is* Option A+ with a tier label. The ADR treats this as a waypoint to the full design; it should be treated as a **candidate terminal design** that the capability layer must *earn its way past* with discrimination evidence.

## What the ADR gets RIGHT (credit where due)
- The **structural diagnosis is first-rate** and well-grounded: 82% boilerplate, inert cost_rank, list-order-as-hidden-priority, 2 fake-failover pools, 3 unlinked curated lists, the SR-6 inert precedent. All independently confirmed in the grounding docs.
- **Tier-as-set instead of hand-listed membership** is the correct core idea and genuinely kills the boilerplate + INV-P0 violation.
- **Dark-launch → shadow-validate (Phases 1–2)** before any routing change is exactly right and is the part to keep verbatim.
- Naming **SR-6 as the risk to beat**, and demanding executable gates, is the right instinct — the gates just need to test *effect on decisions*, not *field presence*.
- **Rejecting SaaS routers** as a foundation is correct and well-argued.

## Bottom line
Ship the structure (tiers-as-sets, real cost_rank once pricing is located, live-health ledger, generated views, the 4-phase safety discipline). **Do not** ship the per-work-class capability engine (grades columns + Arch-Router) as core Phases 1–2 — it is inert on today's data, gated on a benchmark (v2) that isn't built and coverage that may never arrive, bridged to public benchmarks that don't map to S0–S6, and its proof-of-effect gate can't even detect its inertness. Re-sequence: build Option A+ (the wrongly-rejected cheaper design) first; make the capability layer *earn* its way in behind a decision-differentiation gate and real benchmark-v2 coverage. That inversion of the recommended decision is why this is REWORK, not SHIP-WITH-FIXES.
